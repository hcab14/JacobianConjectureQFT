# The Missing Observables: an Exact Structure Theorem for im F*

*(2026-07-22. Verified by `scripts/missing_observables.py` (~15 s, exact
sympy; every displayed identity is asserted there). This upgrades the
qualitative statement of `docs/QFT_IMPLICATIONS.md` §4.3(a) — "F\* is a
monomorphism but not an automorphism of the observable algebra" — to an
explicit description of what is missing, with generators, a normal form,
a decision procedure, and the precise algebraic reason there is no
finite-module version.)*

## 0. Setting

$F^*: \mathbb{C}[a,b,c] \to \mathbb{C}[x,y,z]$, $F^*\mathcal{O} =
\mathcal{O}\circ F$, is the pullback along the Alpöge–Mathew map. It is
injective (F is dominant) and not surjective (F is 3-to-1). Question: *what
exactly is the cokernel — which observables cannot be written as functions
of the redefined field?*

## 1. The field-level answer is completely explicit

**Theorem (verified).** Let $K = \mathbb{C}(a,b,c) \hookrightarrow
L = \mathbb{C}(x,y,z)$ via $F^*$. Then:

1. **$L = K(x)$** — adjoining the single coordinate $x$ gives everything:
   $$y = \frac{-B(x, F)}{2\,D_0(F)}, \qquad z = \frac{-D(x, F)}{8\,D_0(F)}$$
   are *identities in* $\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]$ after substituting $a \mapsto
   F_1$, etc. (the fiber parametrization of `jcqft/fibers.py`, read
   backwards).
2. **$[L : K] = 3$ with basis $\{1, x, x^2\}$:** $x$ satisfies the
   eliminant cubic $p(F)\,x^3 + q(F)\,x + r(F) = 0$, and the cubic is
   irreducible over $K$.
3. **Normal form.** Every polynomial observable $\mathcal{O}(x,y,z)$ is
   *uniquely*
   $$\mathcal{O} \;=\; c_0(F) \;+\; c_1(F)\,x \;+\; c_2(F)\,x^2,
   \qquad c_i \in \mathbb{C}(a,b,c),$$
   computable by one polynomial division (reduce mod the eliminant). The
   $c_i$ have denominators that are powers of $D_0 = 27ac^2 - 9bc + 8$
   (fiber parametrization) times powers of $p$ (leading coefficient of the
   eliminant, from the division).
4. **Missing-observable criterion:** $\mathcal{O}$ is a function of the
   redefined field alone iff $c_1 = c_2 = 0$. So the missing observables
   are, at field level, the **free module generated by $\{x, x^2\}$ over
   $\operatorname{im} F^*$** — precisely the two sheet-separating degrees
   of freedom of the degree-3 covering ($S_3$ monodromy: one trivial
   summand = the traces, one 2-dimensional standard summand = the
   separators; cf. `docs/WALL_COMPLEMENT.md`).

Worked examples (all asserted):

| $\mathcal{O}$ | $(c_0, c_1, c_2)$ |
|---|---|
| $x$ | $(0,\ 1,\ 0)$ |
| $x^2$ | $(0,\ 0,\ 1)$ |
| $F_1$ | $(a,\ 0,\ 0)$ |
| $F_3^2 - 5F_2$ | $(c^2 - 5b,\ 0,\ 0)$ |
| $y$ | $\bigl(\tfrac{81abc^2-72ac-15b^2c+16b}{2D_0},\ \tfrac{3p}{D_0},\ \tfrac{-3(9ac-b)\,p}{2D_0}\bigr)$ |
| $z$ | $c_1 = \tfrac{3(27abc^2+54ac-9b^2c+2b)\,p}{4D_0} \neq 0$, etc. |

Note how $p$ — the non-properness polynomial — appears verbatim in the
separator coefficients of $y$ and $z$: the amount by which a coordinate
fails to be an observable of $F(\phi)$ is proportional to the escape
polynomial.

## 2. Why there is no polynomial (finite-module) version

The basis statement above is field-level (equivalently: valid over the
localization at $D_0\,p$). It **cannot** be promoted to
"$\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]$ is a finite module over $\mathbb{C}[a,b,c]$":

**Theorem (verified, exact escape certificate).** $x$ is *not integral*
over $\operatorname{im} F^*$. Along the curve
$$\phi(T) = \Bigl(T,\; y_0,\; \frac{2T - 3T^2 y_0 - c_3}{T^3}\Bigr),$$
the source escapes ($x = T \to \infty$) while $F(\phi(T))$ converges to
the *finite* target
$$\bigl(y_0^2(1 - c_3 y_0),\; y_0(4 - 3c_3y_0),\; c_3\bigr),$$
which lies exactly on the wall $\{p = 0\}$ (asserted; this is a rational
parametrization of the Jelonek set by $(y_0, c_3)$). An integral element
must stay bounded when its "coefficients" do — so no monic relation for
$x$ over $\mathbb{C}[a,b,c]$ exists.

This is the ring-theoretic face of non-properness: **finite module
$\Leftrightarrow$ proper map** (for these dominant Keller maps), and a
proper Keller map would be injective. Every counterexample to the Jacobian
conjecture *must* have this infinite-module pathology; the normal form of
§1 is the best possible structure statement.

## 3. Deciding membership, and the cheapest certificate

- **Field-level / localized:** compute the normal form (one division);
  membership iff $c_1 = c_2 = 0$. Fast and exact.
- **Strict polynomial membership** $\mathcal{O} \in \mathbb{C}[F_1,F_2,F_3]$
  is decidable by the standard tag-variable Gröbner test
  ($\mathrm{GB}(a - F_1, b - F_2, c - F_3)$ in lex, reduce $\mathcal{O}$);
  on this ideal the basis computation is too slow to be useful, and for
  the coordinates it is unnecessary:
- **Fiber-separation certificate (verified):** the exact fiber over
  $F(1,2,3)$ consists of 3 points whose $x$-, $y$-, and $z$-coordinates
  are *pairwise distinct*. Any element of $\operatorname{im} F^*$ (even of
  its fraction field, where defined) is constant on fibers — so none of
  $x, y, z$ is a function of the redefined field. One fiber computation
  settles all three coordinates.

## 5. QFT reading

The "algebra of observables of the redefined field" $\operatorname{im}
F^*$ has *corank 2 as a module* inside the full observable algebra, with
explicit generators $x, x^2$ for the quotient — these are the operators
that distinguish the three classical vacua over a source $J$. They are
precisely the observables that perturbation theory around one vacuum
constructs as *algebraic functions* of $J$ (branches of the cubic), and
that no polynomial (or entire) function of $J$ represents. The
obstruction to representing them is quantitative: their separator
coefficients carry the factor $p$, and they cease to exist as finite
quantities exactly where $p = 0$. "Which observables are missing" and
"where does the theory degenerate" have the same answer.
